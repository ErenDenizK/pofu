# Subtext checklist

A list to look through after writing, not a mold to write into. A "no"
is a prompt to reread the line; a plain line that a person would really
say plainly can stay. The writer decides.

## The scene

- [ ] The **forbidden honest line** from the card appears nowhere, in no
      synonym, in no other character's mouth, in no stage direction.
- [ ] One **object** carries the feeling, and the change in the scene is
      visible in what happens to that object.
- [ ] The scene's change is readable **with the dialogue removed** (mute
      test: watch the stage directions only).
- [ ] A reader can feel something **nobody said**. Name it in the trace;
      if you cannot name it, there is no subtext, only vagueness.
- [ ] Nothing below the waterline (lore bible) is needed to understand a
      line.
- [ ] The ending is an object or a short line; it is not a hook, a moral
      or the theme word.

## Every line

- [ ] It does something **to** the other speaker (asks, refuses, gives,
      teases, stalls, tests). Lines that only inform are cut.
- [ ] No character **names their own feeling**; no character explains
      another's.
- [ ] No line **explains the metaphor or the joke** after making it
      (draft 0's "Öyle bir sessizlik." after the fridge image).
- [ ] At least one answer is **slightly to the side** of its question.
- [ ] **Tag-swap test:** swap two speakers' names. If the lines still
      read right, one voice is not a voice yet.
- [ ] Under eight words, unless the length is the joke.

## Voices (lore bible §3, §4)

- [ ] **Pofu:** feelings go into his cheeks and body; self-observation,
      not commentary; never lectures; at most one "!" per screen.
- [ ] **Each resident:** speaks through their tell and want (Mochi asks
      "açık mı, demli mi?", never "nasılsın"; Damla's glass; Yosun
      apologises for being early).
- [ ] Residents sound like **a person you could meet** (an aunt at a çay
      ocağı), not like a trait.

## Across scenes

- [ ] The primary **device** differs from the last scene with these
      characters, and the two variants differ from each other.
- [ ] A **callback** echoes an earlier habit with one change, or the
      scene plants one for later.
- [ ] No device appears three times in a row in a batch.

## Turkish

- [ ] Spoken register; a person over 50 and a person under 20 both hear
      it as normal.
- [ ] No calques (narrative-craft §3.6), no spliced suffixes, vowel
      harmony checked by ear.
- [ ] Particles (ya, işte, ki, de) are the character's, not filler, and
      no particle ends more than one line in three.
