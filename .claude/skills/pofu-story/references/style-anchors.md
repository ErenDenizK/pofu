# Style anchors

Real human writing in the register we want. Read before drafting and
match the **move** each anchor makes, never its words. Quotes are a line
or two, for study; buy or borrow the books. Lines marked _(verify)_ are
quoted from memory and must be checked against the book before anyone
treats the wording as canonical.

**The founder and friends extend this file.** The best anchors are not
literary: a line your aunt says at the tea garden, a friend's text that
made you laugh. Add them in the last table (no names, nothing private).

## Turkish

| Source | Anchor | The move |
|---|---|---|
| Orhan Veli, "İstanbul'u Dinliyorum" | "İstanbul'u dinliyorum, gözlerim kapalı; / Önce hafiften bir rüzgâr esiyor;" | A feeling built only from sounds, listed plainly. Our whole premise is listening |
| Orhan Veli, "Kitabe-i Seng-i Mezar" | "Hiçbir şeyden çekmedi dünyada / Nasırdan çektiği kadar;" | A whole life told through one small body complaint. Grief made funny by the body: Pofu's cheeks |
| Orhan Veli, "Bedava" | "Bedava yaşıyoruz, bedava; / Hava bedava, bulut bedava;" | Spoken rhythm, repetition, everyday words, irony with no explanation |
| Sait Faik, "Semaver" | (no quote; read the story) _(verify)_ | Love between a mother and son carried by the morning tea and the samovar; nobody says it |
| Sait Faik, "Haritada Bir Nokta" and the Burgazada stories | (read) | Islanders and fishermen noticed precisely; the ending turns on a small gesture |
| Nasreddin Hoca, "Kazan doğurdu" | The pot "gave birth"; later the pot "died" | Deadpan logic inside an everyday ritual (borrowing a pot). The joke is never explained |
| Everyday ritual speech | "Kolay gelsin." / "Sağ ol." · "Ellerine sağlık." / "Afiyet olsun." · a teaspoon laid across the tea glass: "no more" | Ritual phrases that carry warmth with no feeling-words. Every Turkish player decodes them instantly |

## English

| Source | Anchor | The move |
|---|---|---|
| Arnold Lobel, _Frog and Toad Together_, "Cookies" | "What is will power?" asked Toad. "Will power is trying hard not to do something that you really want to do," said Frog. _(verify)_ | Stakes the size of a cookie; the theme said by a character who then fails at it. Pofu's whole subject in a children's book |
| Arnold Lobel, _Days with Frog and Toad_, "Alone" | Frog's note: "Dear Toad, I am not at home. I went out. I want to be alone." _(verify)_ | A sentence that sounds sad and is not; the story is about the gap |
| Ernest Hemingway, "Hills Like White Elephants" | "They look like white elephants," she said. "I've never seen one," the man drank his beer. | An argument about something unnamed, conducted about hills and drinks |
| Tove Jansson, _Moominvalley in November_ | (read) | Odd neighbours who each want one small specific thing; melancholy without despair |
| A Short Hike; Night in the Woods | (play) | Characters slightly wrong about things; written like friends texting |
