# 0024. Shipped plate layers are HEIC q70 with alpha

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-11

## Context

Plate-first islands (ADR-0022, 0023) ship full-size plates, islands and
occluders as PNG with alpha. Home's 11 states weigh 64 MB, mostly 3.6-5.7 MB
plates (2048 x 3072); ~280 cells projected across the islands is ~1.6 GB in the
repo and the download. The founder is sensitive to resolution, so the answer
cannot be fewer pixels.

## Decision

Plate, island and occluder layers (`world-<island>-plate|island|occ-*`) are
**HEIC, quality 70, with alpha** (pillow-heif in `tools/plate/ingest.py`, via
`tools/plate/heic.py`). Backdrops stay JPEG (no alpha). Asset catalogs take
`.heic` natively (iOS 11+); the app loads by asset name, so no Swift changed.

Measured (home day L10, night L10, day L2 plates, 2048 x 3072 RGBA): q60 =
243-452 KB against 3.6-5.7 MB PNG, SSIM .986-.992, ΔE2000 p95 1.1-2.2, invisible
at 200%. We take q70 for headroom. Shipped result for home and Çağlayan (92
files): 120.9 MB -> 18.4 MB; plates 102.8 -> 14.8 MB (ΔE2000 p95 1.4-2.0);
small islands and occluders measure worse (p95 3-8) because 4:2:0 chroma
blurs their saturated edges, which are drawn small or sit on the plate.

## Alternatives

- **AVIF:** ~35% smaller again, but iOS 16+ and not an asset-catalog format.
- **WebP:** no catalog support either; no real size win over HEIC.
- **pngquant (palette PNG):** visibly worse on the painted gradients.
- **JPEG:** has no alpha.
- **Lower resolution:** rejected, resolution is the point.

## Consequences

- Masters and takes (`design/art/`) stay as they are; HEIC is a delivery format
  only, regenerated by ingest. PNGs leave the tree (history keeps them).
- The texture budget check measures decoded size and is unchanged.
- `pillow-heif` joins the plate tool requirements and CI installs it.
- HEIC is lossy: never re-encode a shipped HEIC; re-ingest from the source.
- On-demand resources for non-home islands remain a later option, not decided
  here.
