## 2. The one lift: the island grows, and the player sees it happen

**What happens today.** The island level is the game's largest reward:
a whole new painting, six times in the trial. It rises in two ways, and
neither has a moment.

- *By a build* (five of six times). The level is derived the instant the
  build commits. The painting cross-fades in 1.4 s
  (`PlateHomeViews.swift:31`) **behind the open station sheet, with the
  camera pushed in on the station**, at the same time as the station's
  own "Seviye n!" burst. The pull-back written for this
  (`pullBackForLevelUp`, `IslandSceneModel.swift:1095`) returns at once
  when a sheet is open or a station is focused, and during a build both
  are true. The player closes the sheet onto an island that is already
  different.
- *Overnight* (IL3 in the trial). The app opens on the new painting. The
  beat that would say so exists (`VisitNews.stageGrew`, "Look: the island
  grew overnight!", `VisitQueue.swift:7`) but nothing feeds it:
  `AppModel.beginWorldVisit` (`App/AppModel.swift:345`) builds
  `VisitInputs` without `news`. On day 3 the island has gained a bridge,
  a second isle and a sunflower field, and Pofu says his cheeks are huge.

**Why this one.** Everything the player does (skip a reach, collect,
build) points at the line "Ada n için". When n arrives, the best
paintings in the build change places unseen. The art is done and paid
for; the missing part is two seconds of staging and one sentence. No
other change gives the trial more for less, and it needs no new art.
Onboarding in the painted world and a fuller Today sheet are the next
two candidates; both wait for the trial's answer.

**Before frames.** `path-d02-2300` then `path-d03-0900` (overnight);
`path-d11-0900` then `path-d11-2300` (by a build).

### The brief (builder; Sonnet; high effort, because it moves engine timing)

Branch `feat/island-grows` from `main` **after #430 and #431 merge**
(#431 touches the same scene files). One PR, at most three pushes, one
labelled path-frames run at the end.

**The moment, "Ada büyüdü".** A pure timeline like `BuildMoment`, in a
new `PofuWorld/Moments/IslandGrowMoment.swift`:

| t (s) | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | Any sheet closes and the station focus lets go; the camera travels to `wholeIslandPose`. **The old painting is still on screen** |
| 0.7 | `SoundCue.levelUp` and the `.growth` haptic; a warm bloom rises from the island's centre (reuse `LevelUpBloom`'s light, island-wide) |
| 0.8 to 2.2 | The existing 1.4 s cross-fade to the new painting, now in full view |
| 1.6 | A title card above the island: "Ada n", and under it Pofu's line for that level |
| 4.6, or a tap | The card leaves; the camera settles to the Island stop |

**The one new idea is "the level on screen".** The scene draws the
painting of a *presented* island level that trails the true one until
the moment's cross-fade begins. `HomePlate.resolve` is called with the
presented level (`IslandScene.swift:252`); `noteIslandLevel`
(`IslandSceneModel.swift:294`) starts the moment instead of the bare
pull-back.

- *By a build:* the moment starts at `BuildMoment`'s `sheetBack` cue
  (1.75 s), so the station's burst plays first and whole, then the
  island answers. The sheet does not return to medium.
- *Overnight:* the last presented level is stored in `UserDefaults`
  (the pattern of `cheekLineDefaults`, `WorldHomeModel.swift:92`).
  `AppModel.beginWorldVisit` passes `news: .stageGrew(level)` when the
  world's level is above it. The app lands on the old painting; the
  news beat's button ("See the island", `WorldHome.swift:426`) starts
  the moment. The queue's order is not changed.
- *Reduce Motion:* no camera move and no bloom; the cross-fade and the
  card only. *Low Power:* no bloom.
- A first launch, a restored state and the Prova panel's "Next day"
  jumping several levels: present the true level at once, or play the
  moment once for the last level only. Never queue several.

**Lines.** One line per level, IL2 to IL8, Turkish and English each
written natively by the story desk (section 3, step 2) before the build
starts; the builder receives keys and strings and writes none. Each
names what the player can now see in the painting.
