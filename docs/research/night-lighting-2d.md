# Night lighting for painted 2D sprites (research, 2026-11-06)

Question (founder, 2026-11-06): night looks wrong (global grade + additive ground pools + emissive windows; a hamster beside a lamp is not lit on that side). Wants GI-like night with its own art direction. Tags: **[S]** sourced (title + URL), **[K]** craft knowledge, **[D]** derived for Pofu. Web coverage was thin: almost no shipped game publishes its pipeline; where only press or forum text exists it is said so. Primary engineering sources were not found for Sea of Stars, Eastward, Hollow Knight, Don't Starve, Stardew, Animal Crossing, Unpacking, Spiritfarer.

Pattern [D]: the cheap classes (Stardew, Don't Starve, Hollow Knight) = **grade + additive lights**, which is exactly Pofu's current system and exactly why it looks flat. The "lights affect objects" classes (Sea of Stars, Octopath, Children of Morta) pay for normals/3D scene and per-light shading. Ori shows the premium painted alternative: hand-made directional light data per asset.

## B. "Delit then relit"

1. **Unlight the day albedo.** Baked day art = albedo * (sun + sky) + painted highlights/shadows. Night: multiply by moon ambient so *all* baked sun direction fades. [K]
2. **Moon ambient.** Hue blue-violet (roughly #3A4A8C to #5060A8 at low value), level 12-25 % of day luminance for ground/sprites; saturation low in shadows, **warm light stays saturated** (Purkinje: rods favour blue-green, reds go dark). [S] Kirk & O'Brien, perceptually based tone mapping for low-light https://history.siggraph.org/?p=114629 ; practical CG convention "desaturated, hue shift to blue, sharper lights/shadows" [S] https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=398614 ; caveat that true scotopic vision starts only ~10 lux so strong desaturation is stylistic [S] https://community.acescentral.com/t/all-cats-are-grey-at-night-render-theory-question/4511 . Implement as luminance-driven: desaturate and blue-shift **only dark pixels**, spare bright/lit ones. [S] same ACES thread suggestion, [K].
3. **Per-pixel point lights.** `L = lightColor * intensity * atten(d) * max(dot(N, Ldir), 0)` with Ldir from pixel to light in 2.5D (light given a height z; N from normal map). Falloff: `atten = saturate(1 - (d/R)^2)^2` (smooth finite radius, culls cleanly) or inverse-square with floor. Wrap lighting `(dot+w)/(1+w)`, w≈0.4, avoids hard terminator on soft painted forms. [K]
4. **Avoid double lighting.** The painted sun highlight on the left of a hamster fights a lamp on the right. Options: (a) compress highlights before ambient: `c' = c / (1 + k*max(0, lum-lum0))` or lerp toward a per-sprite mid-tone, k≈0.5-0.8; (b) "unlight" by dividing by a blurred luminance of the sprite (flatten large-scale shading, keep texture): `albedo ≈ c * (blurLum^-s)`, s≈0.4-0.6; (c) painted flat-lit companion (costly; Ori-style hand work). [K]; the 2026-11-03 spike showed derived normals add little by day because light is already painted in, which is *consistent*: derived normals pay off only once day shading is suppressed. [D]
5. **Hemisphere ambient** (cheap GI): `amb = mix(groundBounce, skyColor, N.y*0.5+0.5)`; sky cool blue from above, ground bounce darker and warmer/olive from below. Near lamps, **warm bounce**: add a low-intensity (~15-25 %) non-directional (N-independent) term of the light colour with a wider radius (~1.8-2.5x R) — this is the fake GI that makes lamps feel like they light the room. [K]
6. **Rim/back light.** Light behind a sprite gives a thin edge glow: `rim = pow(1 - saturate(dot(N, V)), 3) * facingLight`, using alpha-distance dome normals: edge pixels have normal pointing out in screen plane, so lighting edges *toward* the source works without any normal map beyond the alpha. Cheap, high payoff, also reads in pixel/painted art. [K]
7. **Colour temperature that reads cosy.** Candle ~1900 K (orange #FF9A3C), lantern/tungsten ~2700 K (#FFB45A), moon ~4100 K rendered as saturated blue (#6F8CFF) (not physically blue; film "day-for-night" convention). [K]; day-for-night convention [S] Kirk & O'Brien URL above. Keep the warm/cool contrast the dominant colour relationship of the scene.

## C. Night art direction

- **Value structure** [K]: nights read by *contrast of a few values*, not by darkness. Typical: sky mid-dark (not darkest), ground/shadow darkest 8-15 % luminance, silhouettes readable by rim and sky behind them, brightest values (lit windows, lamp cores) 90-100 % and small. Keep ~70 % of screen in the two darkest bands, ~5-10 % in bright warm.
- **Colour key** [K]: Ghibli nights (Totoro bus stop, Kiki) are deep blue/teal-violet with warm windows; the ground is *lighter than you think* and cool; Pixar colour scripts push the opposite hue to the key (blue ambient against orange light). Pofu: ambient indigo-violet, accent warm amber, secondary small cyan/pink magic accents only on special objects.
- **Light count** [K]: 2-4 readable light sources per screen; more become noise. "One cosy window" principle: a single lit window/lamp is the emotional anchor, others subordinate (dimmer, smaller).
- **Pools vs objects** [K]: a pool on the ground without lighting the objects in it reads as a decal (Pofu's current problem). Real glow = pool + lit near-side of objects + faint bounce on neighbouring surfaces + light-colour haze/bloom around the source + hard-ish shadow side. Lit objects matter more than a prettier pool. [D]
- **Own night art direction** [D]: treat night as a separate set of grade/ambient/bounce parameters per island palette (colour script), not one global filter.

## D. Mobile cost (iPhone, Metal)

- Per-pixel cost [K]: a sprite fragment shader with 1 albedo + 1 normal tap + K point lights of ~15 ALU each is trivial on A15+ (K=4-8 is fine at 1x-3x pixel density); the cost is **fill rate and overdraw**, not ALU. Count: pixels drawn * K. Cull lights per sprite on CPU by bounding-circle test (light radius vs sprite rect), pass only the 0-4 relevant ones. [D]
- Battery-first [D]: lit result changes only when lights, time of day or the sprite changes. **Cache**: render the lit scene (or lit layers) to one offscreen texture when settled; then 0 Hz. Relight on: light on/off/move, time-of-day step (quantise to e.g. 1 step/min of game time), sprite swap.
- Flicker [D]: animate only the light intensity/radius (candle: ±6 %, 5-8 Hz noise sampled at 30 Hz); affected area relit by re-running the lit pass on the ~tiles that overlap, or on the whole cached layer if cheap. Never animate unlit areas.
- SwiftUI Canvas vs Metal [D]: Canvas stitchable shaders can do the lit shading per layer with light data as uniforms; arrays of K lights as `float4` constants. The spike on normal-map relighting already proved the plumbing. Keep K ≤ 6, a light budget per screen of ≤ 8 with distance culling.
- Memory [D]: normal map at the sprite's res as RGB (or RG + reconstruct z); emissive as a single-channel mask; ASTC. Alternatively pack: alpha channel of normal map = emissive.

## E. Recommendation for Pofu (lead decides)

**Night pipeline (per layer/sprite, composited in this order)**
1. **Flatten day (unlight).** `albedo = c / (1 + 0.6*max(0, lum-0.55))` highlight compression, then divide by blurred luminance^0.4 (large-scale shading only; blur radius ≈ 10-15 % of sprite size, precomputed and baked into a second channel by the tool).
2. **Moon ambient.** multiply by `moon = (0.20, 0.26, 0.45) * 0.55` (luminance ≈ 15-20 % of day), then luminance-masked desaturation: `sat *= mix(0.45, 1.0, smoothstep(0.2, 0.6, lum))`; shadows pushed toward blue-violet by 10-15 %.
3. **Hemisphere term.** `amb = mix(bounce(0.18,0.14,0.16), sky(0.20,0.26,0.45), N.y*0.5+0.5)` (replaces flat moon colour; ground bounce warmer/darker).
4. **Point lights (K ≤ 4 per sprite).** `rgb = Lcolor * I * atten * wrap(N·L, w=0.4)`; `atten = saturate(1-(d/R)²)²`, light height z ≈ 0.35 R above plane; N = normalize(mix(0,0,1 flat, bakedN, strength 0.6)) — strength 0.5-0.7, tuned per class (characters 0.7, props 0.5, architecture 0.35, foliage 0.4). Colours: lantern #FFB45A I=1.4, candle #FF9A3C I=1.0, window #FFC070 I=0.9.
5. **Bounce/GI fake.** Per light: non-directional term `0.2 * Lcolor * I * saturate(1-(d/(2.2R))²)²` added to *albedo-tinted* surfaces (multiplied by albedo, so it reads as coloured bounce, not wash).
6. **Rim light.** `rim = 0.8 * Lcolor * pow(1 - N.z, 2.5) * saturate(dot(Nxy, -toLightXY)... facing)` only for lights behind/side of sprite; cap rim intensity so it never exceeds lit face.
7. **Emissive.** Windows/lanterns: `+ emissiveMask * Ecolor * 1.2`, not multiplied by the ambient; slight bloom (blurred copy 1/4 res, strength 0.25) from lights only. Ground pool: keep the current additive pool but multiply by ground albedo (so it picks up cobbles) and add the warm haze.
8. **Grade (last, mild).** Global LUT only for final taste (contrast, a touch of filmic highlight roll-off), no longer doing the job of colouring everything.

**Assets needed.** (a) Normal maps from our tool (alpha-distance dome + low-pass luminance detail), strength per class as above; (b) blurred-luminance companion (baked, same tool); (c) emissive masks for windows/lamps/glow plants (painted or thresholded from yellow/white lights; Codex can paint masks per object); (d) 1-2 hand-painted night key frames per island (colour script, used as target for tuning the numbers above, not shipped), plus optionally painted night variants only for hero objects (house, lighthouse) where Ori-style painting beats computation; (e) soft light cookies (512 px radial masks, window-shaped, lamp-shade shaped) to shape pools.

**Stays painted vs computed.** Painted: albedo, emissive masks, hero night variants, sky and water. Computed: ambient, lamp light on objects, bounce, rim, flicker, time-of-day interpolation.

**Risks.** (1) Derived normals look plastic/"embossed" on soft painted art (Ori's artists said scripted extraction looks cheap and plasticky [S] https://www.thegamer.com/ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-artists-hand-painted-30000-light-maps/): keep strength ≤ 0.7, rely on wrap lighting, hemisphere and rim rather than detailed normal bump, and hand-fix hero objects. (2) Double lighting if highlight compression is too weak or too strong (flat grey sprites): tune on 5 reference sprites first. (3) Cost: pay per-layer shading; mitigated by caching to one image when settled. (4) Asset-count blow-up if per-asset hand work is needed (Ori scale: 30,000 maps); budget only hero assets. (5) Colour-calibration time: expect several iterations on the founder's iPhone; keep every number in config. [D]
**Suggested pilot (smallest step):** one island at night with 2 lamps + 1 window: steps 1-4 plus rim and emissive on hamster, lamp, house; compare against current system and a painted night key; ship the winning parameters as config.
