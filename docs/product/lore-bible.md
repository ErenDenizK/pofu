## 0. How to read this

The bible has two layers, on purpose (the iceberg rule,
[narrative-craft §3.4](../research/narrative-craft.md#34-the-iceberg-method-for-lore)):

- **Above the waterline:** what the player may ever see or hear.
- **Below the waterline (writers only):** the full answer, so that what
  we leave out is felt, not hollow. **Never put a below-the-waterline
  sentence in the game.** If a line needs one to make sense, the line is
  wrong.

Delivery constraints from the product, which the story obeys:

- Pofu speaks only when the user acts (a reach, a tap, a visit). No
  notification ever speaks for him.
- One line per island visit; story beats arrive with stages (five per
  island) and at the end of a set; rumours never name a date.
- No exposition. The myth is said **once**, on the vapur's first run
  ([archipelago §9.4](archipelago-system.md#94-the-myth)). Everything else
  is shown in objects, weather, and a resident's habit.

## 1. The premise

### Above the waterline

> **Adalar sessizliğe yanaşır.**
> _Islands come in to quiet, the way a ferry comes in to a pier._

The sky sea is full of islands. They stay close only while they can hear
each other. When it got loud, they could not, and they drifted apart.
When it is quiet on Ayçiçeği for long enough, an island out in the fog
hears it, turns, and comes in, one at a time.

Why the verb matters: _yanaşmak_ is what a vapur does at an iskele.
Every Turkish player has watched a ferry come in slowly and bump the
tyres on the pier. The premise is not a metaphor about mindfulness; it is
a ferry docking, which is why the harbor is where every island arrives.

> **[ASK]** Three candidates for the one spoken line. (a) "Adalar
> sessizliğe yanaşır." (b) "Ses kesilince adalar yanaşır." (c) "Sessiz
> olunca gelirler." (a) is the motto; (c) is how Pofu would actually say
> it. My pick: the motto lives on the map's title, and Pofu says (c).

### Below the waterline

Islands are not pulled by anything magical. They drift on the sky's
currents like boats, and **sound pushes them** the way a wake pushes a
moored boat off its pier. Where it is loud, everything is pushed apart a
little, constantly. Where it is quiet, the currents settle and things
float back together. The user's calm is literally the still water that
lets an island come in. Nobody chose this; it is how the sky works.

## 2. The noise and the fog

### Above the waterline

The outer sky is fog (_pus_). It hums, low and steady. You notice it
only when it stops, the way you notice the fridge only when it clicks
off at night and the kitchen is suddenly very quiet.

- Nobody makes the fog. It has no face, no voice, no lair, no plan.
- It never advances. It only thins, and what it shows stays shown
  (the fog only recedes; archipelago §9.2).
- Pofu is not afraid of it. He listens to it.

### Below the waterline

The hum (_uğultu_) is the sum of everything left on: every radio in an
empty room, every kettle left boiling, every screen glowing on a
bedside table after its person fell asleep. No single thing is loud;
together they never stop. The fog is what the sky looks like where the
hum is thick. It is not evil, it is **left on**, which is why the cure
is not a fight but someone switching one thing off.

**Rule:** never make the fog a villain, never call it "the algorithm",
never show phones inside the world. The user's phone is the world's
fridge: never named, only felt when it goes quiet.

> **[SPICE]** The fridge image is the agent's. It works because every
> household has the moment. A human should test whether the image lands
> in Turkish (_buzdolabı susunca_) or whether a better everyday hum
> exists: the motor of a dolmuş at a red light, the TV left on at
> grandma's.

## 3. Pofu's story

### Why a hamster

Because hamsters run. A pet hamster can run a wheel for hours a night
and go nowhere. Everyone knows that image without being told; the
endless feed is a wheel, and **Pofu is the hamster who stepped off**
(the brand thesis in [pofu-voice §3](../brand/pofu-voice.md#3-who-pofu-is-when-he-talks)).
He is not a guru about it. He is a little embarrassed, and very fond of
his cheeks.

### Above the waterline (what the player can piece together over months)

- Behind the little round door, in the corner of his den, stands an old
  wheel. He keeps it dusted. He never runs in it. If you tap it: "O mu?
  Eskiden." ("That? From before.")
- He hums four notes when he waters the sunflower, and always stops at
  the same place.
- He sleeps with his back to the lighthouse.
- He does not know how long he ran. "Bilmiyorum. Çok." ("I don't know.
  A lot.")

### Below the waterline

Pofu lived in a room where the light never went off. A screen on a
bedside table, always glowing, and a wheel beside it. He ran because the
wheel was there and the room was bright and running felt like going
somewhere.

One night the screen went dark by itself (the battery died; the person
had fallen asleep). The wheel slowed. In the dark Pofu heard two things
for the first time: the person breathing, and his own wheel squeaking.
He stopped running **to hear better**. That is the whole origin: he did
not quit, he got curious.

The four notes he hums are that night: breath, breath, squeak, squeak.
The room was so quiet that the first island heard it and came in.
Ayçiçeği was the first island and Pofu its first passenger. He pulled
his wheel along like a hoop because it was his.

### Want, need, wound, tell

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** (says it) | To hear the rest of the song. He knows four notes. |
| **Need** (never says it) | To sit still without needing what comes next. To be bored and fine. |
| **Wound** (hinted only) | The bright room. Turning his back on steady light; feet that twitch when something squeaks. |
| **Tell** | His cheeks. Every feeling goes into them: seeds when he is happy, air when he is cross, nothing at all when he is listening. |
| **Sounds like** | A little brother who has just discovered he is funny, and is careful not to overdo it. |
| **Never** | Lectures, statistics, "you should", sadness about the user, a speech about the wheel. |

Body humour is his native register, and the founder's favourite line
shows it: **"Yanaklarım kocaman oldu."** More in that key: he loses
count of his own cheek seeds at seven; three boops make him sneeze a
seed into the clouds; wet, his fluff doubles; out of Revontuli's sauna he
is pink and very proud.

> **[SPICE]** Pofu's speech is the most important voice in the game and
> must be written by a person who can hear him. Ask a friend to read
> sections 3 and 7 and then text the founder five things Pofu would say
> while eating. Keep the two that make you laugh out loud.

## 4. The residents

Each resident has one **want** they say out loud, one **need** they
never say, a **wound** we only hint at, and a physical **tell** that
carries all three. Arcs resolve when the want quietly turns into the
need, never in a speech. "Sounds like" names a person you could meet,
not a trait.

### Home (Ayçiçeği and its harbor)

| Resident | Want | Need | Wound (hinted) | Tell | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mochi**, lop bunny, çay ocağı | That everyone who sits down stays for a second glass | To sit down herself | She once ran a crowded tea garden where nobody looked up from the table | Turns each glass so its waist faces you; never asks "nasılsın", asks "açık mı, demli mi?" | An aunt who has run a çay ocağı for thirty years and remembers your order, not your name |
| **Fındık**, hedgehog, workshop | Something to fix | To leave a thing as it is | Everything he made was replaced by a newer one | Things stick to his quills without his knowing: a leaf, a bolt, once a whole simit | A retired repairman with labelled matchboxes of screws |
| **The frogs**, lily pond | To start the evening chorus at the same time | (none; they are a chorus) | (none) | They never start together; one is always early and pretends it was a cough | A neighbourhood choir that has rehearsed for twenty years |

## 5. Five long mysteries

Each is **seeded on Ayçiçeği in the first week**, climbs a few rungs on
the way, and **pays off on a later island**. The player gets the payoff
as an object or a moment, never as an explanation.

| # | Seed on Ayçiçeği | Rungs | Payoff | Below the waterline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M1. The four notes** | Pofu hums four notes while watering and stops at the same place (the home motif, archipelago §11.1) | Çağlayan's mill creaks a fifth note; every new island's first rumour is a bell that knows a little more | **Çanlar**, stage 5: Zil's oldest tune, rung to call islands home, begins with Pofu's four notes. Rules for mysteries:

- One open question at a time in the player's head is enough; never
  stack rungs from two mysteries on one day.
- A payoff must be **an object or an image first**, a line second, and
  never more than one line.
- If a payoff needs a sentence from below the waterline to land, redesign
  the payoff.

> **[ASK]** M4 implies a previous keeper. Does that feel warm (someone
> cared for this place before you) or sad (someone left)? My
> recommendation: keep it, because the letters are practical and funny,
> not mournful. A friend's gut reaction decides.

## 6. Tone rules

1. **Cozy stakes.** The biggest thing that can go wrong is that the tea
   gets cold. No danger, no deadlines, no loss that cannot come back.
2. **No villain, and never the user.** The fog is left on, not evil. The
   app is a rival only in Pofu's jealous play (voice §4.1), never in the
   lore. Nobody in the world shames anyone.
3. **Humour from the body and the everyday.** Cheeks, quills, sneezes,
   late mail, tea orders, a goat. Not memes, not formats, not puns that
   need the internet.
4. **Specific beats pretty.** A glass turned so its waist faces you, not
   "a warm welcome". If a line could be in another cozy game, cut it.
5. **Say less than you know.** The iceberg. A character never names their
   own feeling; another character never explains it for them.
6. **Pauses are content.** Scenes leave beats where nothing is said
   (Miyazaki's _ma_).
7. **Restraint in reactions.** Pofu's surprise is "...Musluk değilmiş.",
   not a gasp. One "!" per screen at most.
8. **Endings are quiet and complete.** A scene ends on an object or a
   short line, never a cliffhanger that pulls you back (the guardrail
   against a new reflex, archipelago §6).
9. **Leaving is allowed.** Residents go and come back (Lale, Nazlı). Pofu
   never dies (ADR-0017); nobody else does either.
10. **Turkish first, written natively.** Whole lines, never spliced
    suffixes; spoken register; a person over 50 and a person under 20
    should both hear it as normal.
11. **Never in the world:** phones, screens, feeds, apps, "digital",
    "detox", "mindful", health claims, seeds as a price for screen time.

## 7. Five sample scenes (draft, Turkish with English glosses)

Stage directions are in Turkish too, so a reader hears the scene's pace.
Each scene is short enough to play in under a minute. Glosses are
literal enough to follow, not translations for release.

### 7.1 The first wonder island arrives (Çağlayan, day 4, morning)

```
Sabah. İskelenin ucu. Pofu'nun kulakları dik.

POFU:   Duydun mu?

        (Hiçbir şey. Sonra bir şey: su.)

POFU:   Biri musluğu açık unutmuş.

        (Sis aralanıyor. Beyaz basamaklar, mavi havuzlar. En alttaki
        basamaktan su dökülüyor; aşağı, bulutların içine. Adanın
        boyundan uzun.)

POFU:   ...Musluk değilmiş.

        (En alt basamakta bir su samuru. Elinde bir çay bardağı.
        Bardağı şelalenin altına tutuyor. Doluyor, taşıyor. İçiyor.
        Yeniden tutuyor.)

DAMLA:  Bir dakika. Az kaldı.
POFU:   Neye?
DAMLA:  Hepsine.
```

_Gloss._ Morning, the end of the pier, Pofu's ears up. "Did you hear
that?" Nothing; then water. "Someone left the tap on." The fog parts:
white terraces, blue pools, water pouring off the lowest one into the
clouds, longer than the island is tall. "...Not a tap, then." An otter
on the lowest step holds a tea glass under the fall; it fills,
overflows; she drinks it and holds it out again. "One minute. Almost
there." "Almost what?" "All of it."

> **[SPICE]** "Biri musluğu açık unutmuş" carries the scene and also
> plants the fog's "left on" idea. Is it funny or just cute? Damla's
> voice is unknown: give her one verbal habit a friend actually has.

### 7.2 Gezgin's first night (day 8)

```
Gece. Herkes yatmış. Fener dönüyor.

        (İskelenin direklerinde bulut bir yükseliyor, bir iniyor.
        Yavaş. Çok yavaş.)

POFU:   (çay ocağının kapısına, fısıltıyla) Mochi. Uyuyor musun?
MOCHİ:  (içeriden) Uyuyordum.
POFU:   Bulutlar nefes alıyor.
MOCHİ:  Yat, Pofu.

        (Pofu yatmıyor. İskelenin ucuna oturuyor. Sisin en uzak
        yerinde bir ışık açılıyor: yuvarlak, soluk, ay kadar.)

POFU:   Ay doğdu.

        (Işık kapanıyor. Biraz sonra yine açılıyor. Bu sefer Pofu'ya
        bakıyor.)

POFU:   ...Ay iki kere doğmaz.

        (Işık kapanıyor. Bulut direklerde yükseliyor, iniyor. Pofu
        nefesini ona uyduruyor. Bir süre ikisi aynı anda nefes
        alıyor.)

POFU:   İyi geceler.

—

Sabah. İskelede, bir köşesi ıslak bir zarf.

        "Yarın oradayım. Erken gelirsem kusura bakmayın. — Yosun"

        (Damgada üç hafta önceki tarih var.)
```

_Gloss._ Night; everyone asleep; the lighthouse turning. Cloud rises
and falls against the pier's posts, slowly. Pofu whispers at the çay
ocağı's door: "Mochi. Are you asleep?" "I was." "The clouds are
breathing." "Go to bed, Pofu." He does not. At the far edge of the fog a
light opens, round and pale, as big as the moon. "The moon's up." It
closes; it opens again, and this time it looks at him. "...The moon
doesn't rise twice." It closes. Pofu matches his breathing to the cloud
tide; for a while they breathe together. "Good night." Morning: on the
pier, an envelope wet at one corner: "I'll be there tomorrow. Sorry if
I'm early. — Yosun." The postmark is three weeks old.

> **[SPICE]** The whale is never named or drawn in full here; that is
> deliberate. Check that "Erken gelirsem kusura bakmayın" reads as a
> joke about a slow tortoise and not as a typo. Mochi's two lines should
> be the founder's call: she must sound like a real aunt woken up.

### 7.3 An island's resolution (Çağlayan, stage 5)

The answer of Çağlayan: the water falls into the clouds, and the clouds
rain on every island.

```
Ayçiçeği'nde yağmur. Çay ocağının saçağı damlıyor.

        (Damla saçağın altında oturuyor. Bardağını yağmura uzatmış.)

MOCHİ:  Şekerli mi?
DAMLA:  Bu benim şelalem.
MOCHİ:  Şekersiz.
DAMLA:  Bulutlara düşüyordu ya. Buraya yağıyormuş.

        (Bardak damla damla doluyor. Ağzına kadar. Damla içmiyor.
        Masaya, Pofu'nun önüne koyuyor.)

POFU:   İçmeyecek misin?
DAMLA:  Doldu ya.
```

_Gloss._ Rain on Ayçiçeği; the çay ocağı's awning dripping. Damla sits
under it, holding her glass out to the rain. Mochi: "Sugar?" Damla:
"This is my waterfall." Mochi: "No sugar, then." Damla: "It was falling
into the clouds. Turns out it rains here." The glass fills drop by drop,
to the brim. She does not drink it; she sets it on the table in front of
Pofu. "Aren't you going to drink it?" "It's full."

> **[SPICE]** This is the arc in one object: want (catch it all) turns
> into need (enough). "Doldu ya" must stay short. Mochi's deadpan
> "Şekersiz" is the agent's best guess at body-and-everyday humour;
> a reader will know in one second whether it lands.

### 7.4 A quiet late-night moment (the user opens Pofu at 01:12)

```
Gece 01.12. Herkes uyuyor. Pofu yuvarlak kapının önünde,
battaniyesine sarılmış.

POFU:   Sen de mi?

        (Bir kurbağa, bir kere. Sonra hiç.)

POFU:   Dinle.

        (Ayçiçeği gıcırdayarak dönüyor. Güneşe değil, sise doğru.)

POFU:   Buzdolabı susunca anlarsın ya, meğer hep çalışıyormuş.
        Öyle bir sessizlik.

POFU:   Yanaklarımda dokuz çekirdek var. Sayarsam uyurum dedim.
POFU:   Yedide kaldım. Hep yedide kalıyorum.

        (Esniyor. Yanakları bir an kocaman oluyor.)

POFU:   Hadi. Sen de yat. Ben buradayım.
```

_Gloss._ 01:12; everyone asleep; Pofu at the round door wrapped in his
blanket. "You too?" One frog, once; then nothing. "Listen." The
sunflower creaks as it turns: not to the sun, toward the fog. "You know
when the fridge stops and you realise it was running the whole time?
That kind of quiet." "I've got nine seeds in my cheeks. I thought if I
counted them I'd fall asleep." "I got to seven. I always stop at seven."
He yawns; his cheeks go huge for a second. "Go on. You go to bed too.
I'm right here."

> **[SPICE]** Plants two mysteries (M3, the sunflower; the fog's hum)
> without saying either. The risk is the fridge line being one sentence
> too explanatory; try cutting "Öyle bir sessizlik." first. The scene
> must end by sending the user to bed, never by inviting them to stay.

### 7.5 A parting gift (Leylek leaves in autumn)

```
Sonbahar. Rüzgâr döndü. Leylekler kurdeleleri gerdi.

LALE:   Yarın yokuz.
POFU:   Biliyorum.

        (Lale yuvaya bakıyor. Her şey bağlanmış, toplanmış.)

LALE:   Sana bir şey bırakacaktım. Ama her şeyi götürüyoruz.

        (Kanadından bir tüy çekiyor.)

LALE:   Ay.

        (Tüyü uzatıyor.)

LALE:   Kalem yap. Bana yazarsın.
POFU:   Adresin ne?
LALE:   (düşünüyor) Güney.

—

Sabah. Gökyüzü boş. Yel değirmeninin tepesinde boş bir yuva.

        (Pofu tüyü kapının yanındaki rafa koyuyor. Yuvaya bakıyor.
        Rafa bakıyor.)

POFU:   Baharda.
```

_Gloss._ Autumn; the wind has turned; the storks pull their ribbons
taut. Lale: "We're gone tomorrow." Pofu: "I know." She looks over the
nest: everything tied down, packed. "I was going to leave you something.
But we're taking everything." She pulls a feather from her own wing.
"Ow." She holds it out. "Make a pen. You'll write to me." "What's your
address?" She thinks. "South." Morning: an empty sky, an empty nest on
the windmill cap. Pofu puts the feather on the shelf by the door, looks
at the nest, looks at the shelf. "In spring."

> **[SPICE]** "Ay." (ow) is the body beat that keeps a goodbye from
> getting sentimental. "Güney" is the only echo of M4; nobody should
> notice it on first play. Follow-up beat for later: Pofu gives Yosun a
> letter addressed "Güney"; Yosun says it may take a while.

## 9. Self-critique (the agent's, before any human reads it)

- **The scenes lean on one device:** a small misunderstanding and a
  deadpan correction (the tap, the moon, "Şekersiz"). Three times in
  five scenes is a tic. A human pass should replace at least one.
- **"Below the waterline" is long.** That is correct for a bible, but
  the temptation will be to leak it into stage lines. The rule in
  section 0 has to be enforced in review.
- **The residents table is trait-shaped.** Each row is a hypothesis with
  no lived detail yet; it will not feel human until a friend who loves
  that character adds one thing from their own life.
- **The Turkish is careful, not alive.** It avoids calques and slop, but
  it has nobody's accent. That is exactly the spice the founder and his
  friends are for.
- **Untested length.** None of the scenes has been read aloud with the
  art. Some beats will want to be wordless once animation exists; cut
  lines before adding any.
