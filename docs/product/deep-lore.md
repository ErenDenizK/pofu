("the current story is part of the main story, but behind it I want much

The theme, never said until the last fragment of the last book:
**quiet is a gift, always from someone, to someone.**

## 1. The iceberg

Four layers. Each book opens exactly one of them a little further, and
each layer re-reads the one above it, so old islands keep growing in the
player's head.

| Layer | What the player can know | Opened in | The feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The islands** | Islands drift away when it is loud and come in when it is quiet. Funny residents with small wounds; each island's story ends. A hamster, a tea stall, a cloud-whale | Book One, from day 1; always true | Cozy curiosity |
| **2. The keeper** | A light switched off for someone's quiet is kept by the sky (clear water, aurora, dark stars). Whoever does it is a Bekçi. Ayçiçeği had one: his trail, his glass, his letters. Mochi knew him. Nazlı was his. **The player is the new Bekçi** | Book One (the four main islands; the finale at Mehtap) | Being thanked, not praised |
| **3. The history** | The islands were once one Ring. A slow age called the Long Bright pulled them apart. Seven keepers held the sky from above, one after another; the old Bekçi was the seventh. The stones are pieces of one text. Listener, reader and carrier are roles older than the Ring. Nazlı is the last waking Carrier; some islands are sleeping ones | Book Two and Book Three (one keeper per season; the Söz's middle lines) | Awe at scale, still gentle |
| **4. The heart** | The sky itself is made of quiet that people gave each other. The player is the first keeper **from below** since the Long Bright began. Pofu was the first quiet the player ever gave. The song has a fifth note, and it is the player's | The last book; parts of it may never be said, only felt | "It was always about this" |

Layer 4 is allowed to stay unsaid forever. If a late book cannot show it
in an object and one line, it stays below.

## 2. Cosmology

### 2.1 What the archipelago is

- **Aşağısı (Below).** The loud world. It is never shown except as far,
  small windows seen in water or from Nazlı's back at night. No faces,
  no phones, no screens: only lights, on or off.
- **Gök Denizi (the sky-sea).** Above Below, a sea of air with currents
  and a surface, its **waterline**, on which the islands float like
  moored boats.
- **Durgun Su (the Still Water).** Under the waterline, clear and very
  deep. Every quiet given below sinks here and is kept. This is the
  in-world "below the waterline"; the writers' phrase in other docs
  means the hidden half of the story. Do not confuse the two in briefs.
- **Uğultu (the hum) and Pus (the fog).** The hum is every light left on
  for no one, added together. The fog is what the sky looks like where
  the hum is thick. Neither is anyone's fault, neither has a will.
- **Başıboş fenerler (stray lamps).** Every light left on below shows in
  the fog as a small lit thing drifting with no one holding it. Stray
  lamps hum. They are not evil; they are lonely.

### 2.2 Quiet is not silence

The single rule the whole world turns on: **quiet means nothing is left
on for no one.** A waterfall, a bell at dusk, frogs, a kettle someone is
watching, a lamp kept for a traveller: all quiet. A light left burning
in an empty room is noise even when it makes no sound. This is why
Çağlayan's falls may flow again, why Çanlar's bells are not a problem,
and why Pervane's lamp is allowed to stay lit (a light kept for someone
is not a light left on).

### 2.3 What the sky does with a gift

A light switched off for someone's quiet is kept in three forms, which
are **three ages of one gift**:

| Form | When | Seen at | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear water** | The night it is given | Çağlayan's pools; the Still Water | Given |
| **Aurora** | The morning the one it was for wakes rested | Revontuli | Received |
| **Dark star** (_kara yıldız_), a small gap in the sky | Every night after, for good | Mehtap's chart | Remembered |

Given, received, remembered. Revontuli's aurora is brighter after the
player's calm nights because those nights were received by someone. The
sky forgets nothing that was given.

### 2.4 Why islands float and drift

Islands stand on the Still Water. The hum pushes them apart like a wake
pushes a moored boat off its pier ([lore bible §1](lore-bible.md#below-the-waterline)).
What used to hold them together was **song**: the Carriers (§3.1), great
cloud-whales who sang one low tune at dusk, heard through the water the
way whale song carries through the sea. Islands that hear each other
drift together. Islands that cannot hear anything drift off, and each
goes still at the hour its last light was switched off, and keeps that
hour like a photograph (loop 2's hour rule). Ayçiçeği alone runs on the
player's clock, because the keeper lives there and is not a memory.

## 3. Deep history

### 3.1 The ages

| Age | What happened |
|---|---|
| **The First Night** (_İlk Gece_) | Before any island: somewhere below, a hand put out a candle so that a small one could sleep, and hummed while it did. Four sounds: the sleeper's breath, the watcher's breath, the small click of the flame going out, the step away. That quiet rose. It was the first clear water, then the first aurora, then the first dark star, the **Kutup Karası** (the Pole Dark) from which every chart is still drawn. From the breath of that night a cloud calf was born, the first **Carrier**, and she took the four sounds as her name. The hand is never shown or named: **İlk El**, the First Hand |
| **The Ring** (_Halka_) | For a very long age, people below put lights out for each other: parents, night watchmen, old men in tea houses closing the shutters. The quiet rose and built land on the Still Water; the islands stood in one ring. The Carriers, many now, swam the ring at dusk and sang the four notes; Çanlar's ringers rang them; every island answered. The **Readers** carved what keeping is into one ring-shaped stone, the **Halka Taşı**, at the Ring's centre, which was the sunflower's island, Ayçiçeği |
| **The Long Bright** (_Uzun Aydınlık_) | Not an event, an age; nobody can name its first night. Below, lights stopped going out. Not out of malice: light is lovely, and people kept it on longer, then all night, then for no one. The stray lamps multiplied and the hum grew. The Carriers' song could not be heard through it; one by one they lay down to sleep and became islands themselves. Without song the Ring loosened. The Ring Stone cracked along its seams and each island carried its piece into the fog. Çanlar rang to answers that never came |
| **The Lantern Keepers** (_Fenerli Bekçiler_) | When below stopped keeping, a few small ones in the sky took the job up from above. They walked the fog at night with a lantern and put out stray lamps with a cupped paw, one by one; sometimes, far below, that light then went out too, because someone happened to notice. It is slow, tiring work. There were **seven**, each handing the lantern to the next. They held the islands from dissolving, but could not bring them home |
| **Tonight** (the game) | A light went out below that no keeper had touched: someone switched it off themselves. The first keeper **from below** in the whole Long Bright had woken. That is the player |

### 3.2 The seven Lantern Keepers

The Bekçi is a job, never a bloodline. Each keeper became one by doing
it, and each left the sky three things: a trail of dark stars (a faint
constellation on Mehtap's chart), a handover object on Emanet's back
shelf, and a resting place. **No keeper died.** Keepers go to rest
(_dinlenmeye çekilir_); a keeper at rest helps an island hold, the way a
sleeping cat keeps a blanket in place.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Işıl** | firefly | Her own light, so the moths could sleep: a light that learned to go dark | An empty glow-jar | In the heart of Ayçiçeği's sunflower, which grew around her sleep | Last book |
| 2 | **Ayaz** | snow hare | The northern lamps, for the fox kits of Revontuli | One mitten | Under the sauna stone at Revontuli (why it is always warm) | Book Two |
| 3 | **Balaban** | bittern | The mill's lamp at noon, for an otter pup's nap | The mill key | Behind Çağlayan's stopped falls | Book Two (hinted in Book One) |
| 4 | ~~**Gümüş**~~ (dropped from Book One: the map dropped invented names, 2026-11-06; the slot and its objects stay unnamed until Book Two) | magpie | A lamp over the counter, for a tired clerk | A silver thimble | In Emanet's back room, in a drawer with no handle | Book Two |
| 5 | **Ağır** | tortoise | Every lamp on one long road, slowly, for travellers | A postage stamp with no value printed | At the bottom of Gezgin's mail sack, folded small (a joke Yosun does not know) | Book Three |
| 6 | **Pervin** | moth | All lamps but one: she kept one lit for the lost | A wick | In the wax of Pervane's lamp | Book Three |

Why seven, and why it matters: the chart at Mehtap shows **seven faint
trails** and one new, bright one. Book One only follows the seventh. The
other six are years of content: one keeper's story per later book or
season, each re-reading an island the player already loves.

## 4. The characters' hidden truths

### 4.1 The old Bekçi: Ihlamur

- **Who.** A dormouse, in Turkish a _yediuyur_, a "seven-sleeper". The
  irony is the point: the one who stayed awake so others could sleep is
  an animal made for sleeping. His name is the linden tea Turks drink
  before bed. Only Mochi says it, once, in a late book.
- **How he became keeper.** In the Long Bright he was the one dormouse
  in Koke's woods who could not hibernate: it was too bright. He walked
  at night instead. He followed the sunflower, which always turns to the
  newest dark, found Ayçiçeği empty and Pervin's lantern hanging on the
  sunflower's stem, and picked it up. Nobody appointed him.
- **What he did.** For many winters he walked the fog around Ayçiçeği,
  cupped stray lamps, wrote practical letters ("If the door squeaks,
  olive oil"), hummed the four notes without knowing where they came
  from, and drank ıhlamur at Mochi's stall every dusk. Nane said
  "günaydın" to him at sunset; he answered.
- **Nazlı.** He raised her (§4.3).
- **Why he grew tired.** He kept alone. The Söz says a keeper never
  keeps alone: one hears, one reads, one carries (§5, line 8). He had a
  reader (Mochi) and a carrier (Nazlı) but no listener; he could never
  hear the islands, only find them. Walking blind for years is tiring.
- **The handover.** One autumn night a light went out below that he had
  not touched. He understood at once: a keeper from below. He did what
  old night watchmen do when the next one arrives: he went to rest. He
  let his lantern go where it would be found (the map: it is fished up through Revontuli's ice hole at Olta and rides to the finale; it did not hang on Emanet's hook), set his glass upside down on
  Mochi's top shelf, wrote one last letter (to Yosun, the first ever
  addressed to him), and asked Lale to carry him south, where a dormouse
  can sleep a very long winter. He did not say goodbye to Nazlı, because
  she would have followed, and a carrier belongs to the keeper who comes,
  not the one who goes.
- **Now.** Asleep at Fener Kayası, curled in a stork's nest on the
  unlit lighthouse. He will wake **once**, one spring in a later book,
  drink a glass with Mochi, meet Pofu, and go back to sleep. He never
  takes the job back.

### 4.2 Mochi's full secret

1. **She is a Reader.** Readers are the ones who could never hear the
   islands, which is exactly why they wrote everything down. They read
   the **Still Script** (§5) in still water: tea, a bowl, a puddle. Her
   tea ripples near an island because islands ride on song she cannot
   hear but water can show.
2. **Ihlamur taught her to read**, drawing the script in spilled tea on
   her counter on slow nights. She was his reader for years: he found,
   she read. She knows more than Pofu does from the very first day.
3. **Her lost island.** Her wound, "a crowded tea garden where nobody
   looked up", was a real island: **Çınaraltı**, a tea garden under a
   plane tree, the loudest island of the Ring's last years. She was its
   youngest server. One night she carried a tray across to Ayçiçeği for a
   dormouse who sat alone, and while she poured, Çınaraltı drifted off
   into the fog. She stayed. She has never said she misses it.
4. **The glass she misread.** In her stall's custom an upside-down glass
   means "keep it for me". In the keepers' custom, written on the Ring
   Stone, it means "I am tired; pour for the next one". Ihlamur meant the
   second; Mochi read the first and has waited ever since. The Emanet
   fragment (line 7) is the moment she reads it right. That is her turn,
   and why she can sit down at the end.
5. **The ıhlamur tin.** One tin of linden, never opened, on the top
   shelf next to the glass. Her stall only serves çay.

### 4.3 Nazlı: origin, humility, grown form

- **Origin.** When the last Carrier still singing lay down to sleep in
  the Long Bright (she became Koke, §6), she set her calf, a cloud the
  size of a teapot, on Ayçiçeği's harbor, in the steam of Mochi's urn.
  Ihlamur found her there. He fed her on quiet nights and linden steam,
  hung a lantern hook on her back so he could find her in fog, and put a
  pocket of Ayçiçeği's soil on her back so she would always carry home.
  That pocket is the "piece of island" on her back.
- **Her marks.** The hook is visible from day one. The scar shaped like
  a switched-off light is where his lantern hung on the nights he put it
  out so she could sleep. Her breathing slows near slow breathers
  because she was raised by someone who breathed slowly beside her.
- **Why humble.** Nobody told her what she is. She thinks she is a cloud
  that got too heavy. When Ihlamur went south she could not follow, so
  she drifts wherever it is quiet, looking for him. The quiet she keeps
  finding is the player's. She is wilful because she is waiting for
  someone else.
- **Her names.** "Nazlı" is what Ihlamur called her. Her true name is
  sung: it is the four notes, the name every Carrier has carried since
  the First Night.
- **The grown form (end of Book One).** Not a new creature: a Carrier
  awake. She chooses the new keeper (partnership, not obedience), and
  **sings for the first time**: the four notes, low enough to move
  water. It is the first Carrier song in the sky since the Long Bright.
  The soil on her back blooms into a young sunflower (a cutting of home);
  Ihlamur's lantern, fished up at Olta (the map; not Emanet), hangs on the hook, lit, a light
  kept for someone. Far off, Koke breathes once in answer. Nobody
  comments; the book ends on good night.
- **The ceiling.** Each later book may bring a new "edition" of her
  (another detail, never more power): Book Two her song reaches the lit
  window; Book Three she dives into the Still Water; the last book she
  leads the woken Carriers home.

### 4.4 The four-note lullaby

- **Origin.** The four sounds of the First Night: the sleeper's breath,
  the watcher's breath, the click, the step away. They are what quiet
  sounds like whenever one being gives it to another, so every keeper
  hums them without being taught, and the first Carrier took them as her
  name.
- **Why Pofu stops at the same place.** The tune has a fifth note, and
  the fifth note belongs to whoever is keeping now. Pofu cannot sing it
  because it is not his. Ihlamur never found his; he had no listener.
- **Pofu's version.** The night his light went out, the four sounds in
  that room were: the player's breath, Pofu's own breath, the click of
  the hand, and the last squeak of his wheel stopping. **The first note
  of Pofu's song is the player.** Never said.
- **The fifth note (last book).** Derived on device from the player's own
  record (for example, the hour of their first resisted reach mapped to
  a pitch), so it is truly theirs and differs per player. Nazlı sings
  the four notes, Pofu stops where he always stops, and the fifth note
  is played once, from the player's side of the screen.

### 4.5 Pofu's true nature

- **Why he hears islands.** A listener is the one a keeper's first quiet
  was for. The sky gives every keeper one. When the player's hand
  darkened the bright window, Pofu was the one who got to stop running:
  the quiet was **for him**, even though the player thought it was for
  themselves. Things given in quiet rise; he rose, and the sunflower
  turned to him, and Ayçiçeği took him in.
- **The trio.** Pofu hears, Mochi reads, Nazlı carries. The same three
  roles the Söz names (line 8), older than the Ring. The new keeper is
  the first in seven to have all three, which is why this time the
  islands come **home** instead of merely holding.
- **Layer 4 (subtext only).** The quiet you take for yourself is also
  given to someone. Pofu is the proof. Writers may let a player feel it;
  nobody ever says it.
- **The wheel.** It never needed him (lore bible M2 stands).

### 4.6 The player

The eighth keeper and the first from below since the Long Bright.
Every resisted reach puts out a stray lamp near Ayçiçeği by itself,
without a paw. On Mehtap's chart the player's trail is the only one not
drawn by a lantern's path: it is drawn by the player's real quiet days.

## 5. The stones: the Söz

The Ring Stone was a flat ring of pale stone carved by the Readers, with
a hollow at its centre the size of a tea glass's foot. When the Ring
broke it cracked into **twelve pieces**; each drifting island carried
one. The script is the **Still Script** (_Durgun Yazı_): rows of shallow
thumb-pressed hollows. Dry, it is a pattern. Fill it with still liquid
and the ripples settle into words that only a Reader can see. So on
every main island **Mochi pours a glass of tea over the stone**, waits,
and reads one line aloud. That is the turn of the island (the arc
template's "Mochi reads the stone").

The full text is called **the Söz** (the Word, also "the promise").
Twelve lines in four parts. Book One reveals lines 1, 4, 7 and 10, which
read on their own as a keeper's job and point home; later lines re-read
them. **[SPICE]**: these are the agent's drafts; a human must rewrite the
Turkish before any line ships.

| 2 | I | Söndürdüğünü görmez; duyar. | They do not see what they put out; they hear it. | Lit-window island | Two |

How the reveals work on characters, not only on lore:

- **Line 1** (Çağlayan) gives the word _bekçi_ to the player for the
  first time, as a definition, not a person. Pofu decides there must be
  one and wants to thank him.
- **Line 4** (Revontuli) makes the aurora mean something; the player can
  check it against their own calm nights.
- **Line 7** (Emanet) is Mochi's turn: she reads her own custom wrong for
  years and right in one glass. She gives Boncuk the ıhlamur tin on
  purpose (§6), which is both her letting go and Boncuk's wish.
- **Line 10** (Mehtap) is the finale's key: Nane's chart, the trail, home,
  tonight. Mochi turns the old glass upright, pours, and sits.
- **Lines 3, 8 and 9** (Book Two) re-read Book One: the four notes were
  always a name; the duo and the whale were always a trio; Ihlamur did
  not leave. Line 9 is also the gentlest answer the series gives to
  anyone who wonders whether a character has died: they rest.
- **Line 11** is literally Mochi's need. **Line 12** is the theme.

The centre hollow: when all twelve pieces have come home they settle
around Ayçiçeği on the Still Water, and the hollow is the harbor itself.
Home is the centre of the Ring because it is the only island on the
keeper's own clock.

One secret per island, tied to the history. **Fillers never carry lore
the main story needs**: their secrets stay unsaid in Book One and are
re-read by later books, which is how a small filler grows big later.

### 7.1 Rules for revealing

1. **One open question at a time** in the player's head. A book closes
   its own question before it opens the next (Book One: who is the
   Bekçi; Book Two: whose window is lit; Book Three: what is under the
   islands).
2. **Objects first, then at most one line.** A reveal is a thing seen
   (a hook, a mitten, a thimble, a feather), then one line or none.
3. **Nobody explains.** No resident summarises the history. The Söz is
   the only text that states anything, and it states one line per island.
4. **Re-read, do not add.** A good late reveal changes what an old object
   meant, rather than adding a new object.
5. **True to the player.** Wherever a reveal can be built from the
   player's own record (day one, quiet days, calm nights, the first
   resist), it must be, computed on device, and it must never shame.
6. **Fillers stay light.** A filler may hold a seed but never a needed
   step. A player who skips every filler still understands every book.
7. **No cliffhanger.** A book ends on good night. The next question
   arrives later, as an object (Yakamoz), after the ending has landed.
8. **Once per book.** The player is named keeper at most once per book,
   and Nazlı's new edition happens once per book.

## 8. Consistency rules and open choices

### 8.1 What can never be true

1. **Nobody dies.** Keepers rest; Carriers sleep; travellers go south and
   come back. Pofu never dies (ADR-0017). Line 9 is the canon answer.
2. **There is no villain.** Not the fog, not the hum, not Below, not the
   people of the Long Bright, not the player. Light was loved, then left
   on. Nobody is blamed for the drift.
3. **No phones or screens in the world.** Below is only windows and
   lights, far away. "The bright window" is never drawn as a device.
   The hum is never "the algorithm".
4. **Quiet is never forced.** A keeper puts out only lights left on for
   no one, or their own. Nobody switches off another's light while they
   are using it; Book Two's window goes dark by the hand of the one
   inside.
5. **Quiet is not silence** (§2.2). Water, bells, frogs and kept lamps
   are never "the problem".
6. **The Bekçi is never a bloodline.** Pofu is not Ihlamur's heir; Mochi
   and Boncuk descend from roles, not powers. Anyone can keep.
7. **Seeds never buy quiet, screen time, lore or Nazlı's growth**
   (ADR-0015). She grows with the bond and the story only.
8. **The player is never the problem and never a guru.** They are
   thanked, once, not praised.
9. **No romance** between keepers, readers or carriers. Fond, old,
   neighbourly love only.
10. **No power escalation.** Nazlı's grown form is the ceiling of each
    book; later editions add a detail, never a fight or a stronger
    power.
11. **Ihlamur never takes the job back**, and no former keeper competes
    with the player.
12. **The First Hand stays unseen.** No human appears on screen, ever.
13. **Nothing in this file appears as exposition.** If a scene needs a
    sentence from here to make sense, the scene is wrong (lore bible §0).

## 9. Türkçe özet (kurucu için)

Yüzeydeki hikâye aynen kalıyor; altına dört katlı bir buzdağı kurdum.

1. **Adalar** (her zaman): gürültüde açılır, sessizlikte yanaşır; her
   adanın küçük hikâyesi biter.
2. **Bekçi** (Birinci Kitap): söndürülen ışık gökte su, aurora ve kara
   yıldız olarak saklanır: **verilen, alınan, hatırlanan.** Eski Bekçi
   Ihlamur adında bir yediuyur; Mochi onu tanıyordu; Nazlı onundu;
   oyuncu yeni Bekçi.
3. **Tarih** (İkinci ve Üçüncü Kitap): adalar bir zamanlar tek bir
   **Halka**ydı. "Uzun Aydınlık" denen yavaş bir çağda aşağıda ışıklar
   kimse için yanık kaldı; uğultu, adaları birbirine bağlayan
   **Taşıyıcı** balinaların şarkısını bastırdı; balinalar uyuyup adaya
   dönüştü (Koke, Nazlı'nın uyuyan annesi). Yedi "Fenerli Bekçi"
   gökyüzünü yukarıdan, tek tek fener söndürerek ayakta tuttu; Ihlamur
   yedincisi.
4. **Kalp** (son kitap): gökyüzü insanların birbirine verdiği
   sessizlikten yapılmış. Oyuncu, Uzun Aydınlık'tan beri **aşağıdan
   gelen ilk Bekçi**. Pofu, oyuncunun verdiği ilk sessizlik.

**En büyük sırlar:** dört nota bir ninni ve Taşıyıcıların adı (nefes,
nefes, tık, adım); Pofu'nun ilk notası oyuncunun nefesi; şarkının beşinci
notası oyuncunun kendi verisinden çıkıyor. Mochi bir "Okuyucu": taşlardaki
yazıyı çay dökerek okuyor; Ihlamur'un ters bardağını yıllarca "benim için
sakla" diye yanlış okudu, aslında "sıradakine doldur" demekti (Emanet'te
anlıyor, Mehtap'ta oturuyor). Kaybolan çay bahçesi Çınaraltı ileride
dönüyor. Taşlar on iki satırlık tek bir metin, **Söz**; son satırı:
"Sessizlik bir hediyedir; hep birinden birine." Kimse ölmüyor, kötü
karakter yok, dünyada telefon yok.
